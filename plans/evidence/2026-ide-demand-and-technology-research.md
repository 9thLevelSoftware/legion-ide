# 2026 IDE demand, technology, and feature research

**Research cut-off and access date:** 2026-10-08. Primary-source desk research combining developer surveys, original studies, protocol specifications and first-party product documentation. Recommendations are inference, not measured demand for Legion. This report does not authorize implementation or change product-readiness status.

## Recommendation

Build a dependable IDE in which developers can choose assistance, understand proposed changes, verify them efficiently, and retain control. Prioritize reliable editing/debugging and migration, excellent change review, agent/model choice, precise context, and bounded execution. Treat formal verification and new collaboration paradigms as measured experiments rather than assumed mass-market requirements.

## Evidence of demand

Stack Overflow's 2026 survey retained 30,903 responses from 169 countries, fielded June 23–August 5. Recruitment through its own properties and community email makes this a self-selected sample. Question-level denominators differ and must not be replaced with the full survey count. [2026 methodology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/methodology/).

| Signal | Result | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI use at work, 2026 | n=17,464: 65.9% use coding assistants/agents; 26.2% use agents/automated workflows. | Assistance is broadly used; these overlapping categories are not market shares. |
| Conditional trust, 2026 | n=14,304: 48% trust output when easily verifiable; 6.6% trust it for many tasks including important decisions. | Verification deserves first-class UX. |
| Workflow use, 2026 | n=12,547: familiar-code generation 69.3%; debugging/troubleshooting/refactoring 63.8%; tests 58.1%; codebase understanding 49.7%; review 40.5%. | Strongest observed uses center on concrete engineering tasks. |
| Validation behavior, 2026 | n=13,162: 76.5% run output locally; 63.9% compare with codebase context; 9.6% use it as-is. | Integrate execution and contextual review. |

All four rows come from [Stack Overflow 2026 AI data](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data). They describe reported behavior, not causal productivity, willingness to pay, or a ranking of requested IDE features.

**Incumbent gravity, explicitly 2025 carryover:** Stack Overflow's IDE question received 26,143 answers (53.3% of valid responses). VS Code was the most-used and most-desired IDE. The reviewed 2026 technology tables do not supply a comparable IDE ranking. Familiar projects, keybindings, debugging and extensions are therefore sensible migration priorities, but that feature-level conclusion is an inference. [2025 technology results](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology), [2025 methodology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/methodology/).

**JetBrains corroboration and limits:** its 2025 ecosystem survey included 24,534 developers, fielded April–June, with weighting for geography, employment, language and JetBrains usage; residual sponsor/selection bias remains possible. [2025 report](https://devecosystem-2025.jetbrains.com/), [methodology](https://lp.jetbrains.com/developer-ecosystem-2025-methedology/). A September 2026 CEO essay cites newer survey findings and emphasizes review, coordination and governance. Its extracted charts include unrendered zero placeholders and do not expose complete question denominators/methodology, so this report uses it as directional vendor evidence rather than importing its headline percentages into the table. [JetBrains, The Big Handover](https://kairos.jetbrains.com/narratives/the-big-handover).

**Workplace evidence, 2025 carryover:** Google Cloud's DORA announcement describes nearly 5,000 technology professionals, 90% workplace AI use and over 80% perceived productivity improvement. It also reports an association with delivery instability. These are survey/observational findings from a commercial research program, not causal effects of an IDE. [DORA announcement, September 23, 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report).

## Productivity evidence: include review and rework

- **Positive controlled result, 2024:** a trial with 96 Google engineers estimated a 21% task-time reduction from AI. Its confidence interval was wide, and one enterprise task using internal tools limits generalization. [Original paper, October 16, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944).
- **Contrary controlled result, early 2025:** METR randomized AI access across 246 tasks performed by 16 experienced contributors in familiar open-source repositories and measured 19% longer completion time. This is a dated, bounded result, not a claim that today's tools universally slow developers. [Original METR paper](https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf).
- **Important update, February 24, 2026:** METR says its follow-up signal is unreliable because of participant/task selection and measurement problems, including concurrent-agent work. It believes newer tools probably help more than its early-2025 estimate suggests, but cannot reliably quantify the increase from that study. [METR experimental-design update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/).
- **New self-report evidence, May 11, 2026:** METR surveyed 349 technical workers, including 87 software engineers, during February–April. Respondents reported a median 1.4–2x increase in work value; the authors caution about the magnitude. Self-reported value is not measured speed or a universal IDE benefit. [METR survey](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/).
- **Review burden, March 10, 2026:** DORA analyzed 1,110 open-ended responses from Google engineers and describes gains in drafting alongside time spent verifying output. This qualitative sample may be influenced by preceding questions; it is not a controlled net-speed estimate. [DORA analysis and methodology](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/).

**Implication:** evaluate correct, reviewed outcomes, including time spent checking and repairing. Never equate adoption, perceived speed, generated lines or model benchmarks with realized customer benefit.

## Technology and feature assessment

The following ranking is our product judgment, not a survey ranking. "Strong" means direct evidence of an underlying need or established workflow; "competitive" means current vendor investment and availability; "experimental" means research or an unvalidated product hypothesis. A mature building block can be a better choice than a newly announced technology.

### Ranked feature opportunities

| Priority | Feature and user outcome | Evidence and confidence | Recommended technique and acceptance signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Reliable daily editing, language navigation, debugging, tests, Git, accessibility, and recovery | Established IDE baseline; adoption of an incumbent alone does not prove which feature causes retention. [LSP](https://microsoft.github.io/language-server-protocol/), [DAP](https://microsoft.github.io/debug-adapter-protocol/) | Keep native editing responsive; complete actual language/debug journeys. Measure input-to-paint, crash recovery, dirty-text preservation and task completion, not only service benchmarks. |

Competitors now invest in review and delivery workflows. [VS Code agent merge, preview, September 2](https://code.visualstudio.com/updates/v1_136) | Bind evidence to the proposed state and dependencies; distinguish optional from required checks. | P0 | Choose an agent/model without surrendering IDE controls | Competitive convergence: JetBrains and Zed launched an ACP registry in January 2026. This proves an integration ecosystem, not universal protocol coverage. [JetBrains announcement](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/01/acp-agent-registry/) | Add a capability-negotiated adapter and compatibility matrix. | P0 for Legion's target | Private/local or self-hosted operation, enforceable permissions, visible costs and cancellation | Strong strategic fit for Legion's stated enterprise customer; broad willingness to pay remains unmeasured. [Zed local models](https://zed.dev/blog/local-ai-in-zed) | Separate inference location, tool execution location, storage, telemetry and egress. ### New technology worth evaluating now

**1. ACP for editor-to-agent interoperability; MCP for external tools; AHP for shared session hosts.** ACP v1 specifies capability negotiation, session updates, permissions, cancellation and optional file/terminal methods. MCP's security guidance addresses tool-service authorization and trust. Microsoft's August 2026 AHP work addresses multiple clients observing one host-owned session. These boundaries are related but not interchangeable. Recommended for Legion: evaluate ACP first for agent choice, retain scoped MCP integration, and study AHP when persistent multi-client sessions become a real requirement. An external agent must not gain direct writes to the authoritative workspace merely because it speaks a protocol; qualify an isolated staging/proposal adapter and decline unsupported authority mappings. [ACP v1](https://agentclientprotocol.com/protocol/v1/overview), [MCP security guidance, 2026-07-28](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/2026-07-28/tutorials/security/security_best_practices), [AHP architecture](https://code.visualstudio.com/blogs/2026/08/26/agent-host-architecture).

**2. Efficient next-edit prediction rather than only chat completion.** Zed's May 8 Zeta2.1 report describes multi-region edit output and reports p50 response time falling from 189 ms to 136 ms; this is a vendor workload comparison, not a Legion target. Microsoft's September model combines completion, nearby edits and longer-distance suggestions. Recommended experiment: reuse a qualified provider/model and benchmark compact edits against current assistance before considering model training. [Zeta2.1](https://zed.dev/blog/zeta2-1), [Copilot model engineering](https://code.visualstudio.com/blogs/2026/09/16/building-the-github-copilot-inline-suggestions-model-part-one).

**3. XGrammar-2 for structured tool output.** The May 4 release introduces composable Structural Tags, cross-grammar caching and serving integration for constrained output. It is useful infrastructure for avoiding malformed actions. Syntax constraints do not establish correct tool selection, permission, or safe edits. Evaluate this at an explicitly selected serving endpoint; do not add a proprietary decoder to the IDE or silently alter the approved provider profile. [XGrammar-2 release](https://blog.mlc.ai/2026/05/04/xgrammar-2-fast-customizable-structured-generation).

**4. Resolved and incremental context.** Tree-sitter incrementally updates syntax trees; LSP supplies language services; SCIP supplies a portable code-index format used for precise navigation. These are established technologies, not 2026 inventions. Their useful combination is a context system that updates only affected work and explains the identity, source and age of each result. Recommended design: lexical and structural retrieval first, resolved references where available, learned retrieval only when held-out relevance improves. Neither syntax trees nor embedding similarity prove dependency completeness. [Tree-sitter](https://tree-sitter.github.io/tree-sitter/), [LSP](https://microsoft.github.io/language-server-protocol/), [SCIP-based navigation](https://sourcegraph.com/docs/code-navigation/precise-code-navigation).

**5. Real execution containment and observable agent lifecycles.** Zed documents OS-enforced controls but limits their coverage to particular native-agent tools; external agents, language servers and normal terminals are outside that sandbox. VS Code documents separate platform prerequisites and experimental Windows support. Recommendation: expose the actual enforcement boundary and test every launched path, including build scripts, rather than displaying one global “sandboxed” badge. Competitor availability does not qualify Legion's stricter formal-worker policy or justify changing its approved WSL2 route. [Zed sandbox limits](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/sandboxing), [VS Code sandbox prerequisites](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/agents/run/agent-sandboxing).

**6. Artifact-based verification and agent debugging.** Playwright traces combine actions, DOM snapshots, console/network events and source locations. AgentStepper's February 2026 preprint explores breakpoints and stepwise inspection of agent trajectories; its user study has twelve participants, so it is an early signal, not broad demand evidence. Recommended approach: first make existing tests and agent actions inspectable, then pilot controlled stepping for difficult failures. Retention of source-bearing traces remains consented. Browser traces cannot replace native Legion GUI acceptance. [Playwright Trace Viewer](https://playwright.dev/docs/trace-viewer), [AgentStepper preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06593).

**7. Bounded counterexample-guided proof repair.** ExVerus, listed by Microsoft Research at ICML July 2026, uses validated counterexamples to inform proof repair and invariants. This is a specialized research opportunity, not evidence of mass-market demand for formal verification or arbitrary Rust repair. Keep the existing supported-domain experiment behind explicit activation, independent checking and ordinary proposal review. [ExVerus](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/exverus-verus-proof-repair-via-counterexample-reasoning/).

**8. Standard development environments and portable procedural knowledge.** Dev Container metadata describes repeatable development environments; Agent Skills packages workflow instructions and resources loaded on demand. Reuse these formats where they meet product needs, but treat executable setup steps and bundled scripts as code requiring trust and execution policy. Neither format is itself a security boundary. [Dev Container specification](https://github.com/devcontainers/spec/blob/main/docs/specs/devcontainer-reference.md), [Agent Skills format](https://agentskills.io/home).

### Techniques for deciding what actually works

These are proposed evaluation practices, not results:

- Optimize **time to a correct, reviewed change**, including review, repair and CI, rather than generated lines, suggestion acceptance or agent activity.
- Test assisted and manual workflows on comparable real repository tasks; separate task familiarity, language, repository size and developer experience. Report failures and variance, not just averages.
- Include deliberate stale context, dirty buffers, prompt injection, permission denial, model outages and cancellation in acceptance workloads.
- Measure end-to-end latency distributions and memory under editing plus background work. Zed publishes selected, opt-in agent turn-time distributions; those data illustrate observability, not fair model rankings across unmatched tasks. [Zed metrics methodology, April 9](https://zed.dev/blog/agent-metrics).
- Keep one or two bounded workers as the initial orchestration default; add parallelism only when independent tasks improve reviewed throughput at acceptable cost. This is an engineering recommendation, not a proven universal optimum.
- Evaluate retrieval against labeled questions and correct source citations; compare lexical, resolved-symbol and learned approaches independently before combining them.
- Keep privacy controls testable: inference, tool execution, retention and telemetry are separate paths. “Local model” and “self-hosted execution” are not interchangeable with zero egress.
- Validate switching demand with target teams: have them perform their actual edit/test/debug/review workflow and identify blockers, then measure repeated use and willingness to adopt. Survey adoption and release-note frequency are not substitutes.

## Implications for Legion and the existing frontier plan

The repository's [readiness ledger](../product-readiness-ledger.md) defines an enterprise/local-or-self-hosted target and separates substrate evidence from product readiness. Its extension, remote and enterprise gates remain separate obligations. This report is research, not a new readiness assessment or approval to expand those surfaces.

**Reinforced priorities:** the approved review-first journey, F1 resolved impact, F2 freshness/coverage distinctions, F3 inspectable evidence, F6 real cancellation and F8 independent qualification match the strongest practical needs. Keep the native architecture and complete ordinary workflows before layering on frontier mechanisms. See the [confirmed frontier decisions](../../.omp/plans/FRONTIER_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md).

**Additional candidates to evaluate:** an ACP adapter that preserves proposal authority; compact next-edit prediction; a trace-to-diff debugging workflow; portable skills with visible provenance; and explicit migration/extension compatibility reporting. Persistent sessions, Dev Containers and team review belong in their existing remote/enterprise workstreams, not an unapproved parallel platform.

**Keep experimental:** formal proof repair, equality-saturation refactoring, full agent trajectory manipulation, autonomous deployment control and replacing Git/PR collaboration. Current documentation or a research paper establishes feasibility or vendor direction, not a compelling general-purpose IDE purchase reason. The earlier [technology review](technology-review-and-frontier-opportunities.md) remains a historical technical input; do not treat its novelty or capability snapshot as current market validation.

**Recommended sequence:** (1) reliable manual edit/test/debug/Git and migration; (2) optional agent choice plus bounded execution and excellent review; (3) precise impact/context and reproducible test artifacts; (4) target-segment remote/team workflows; (5) measured research pilots. This ordering is a recommendation for future prioritization, not a change to approved scope or shipping authority.

## Research limits and follow-up

This is a primary-source desk review, not a hands-on competitor benchmark, exhaustive market census, security certification, or model leaderboard. Product documents can change and vendor-reported gains may not reproduce on Legion. Mutable documentation was accessed on 2026-10-08; explicitly dated announcements identify their publication date. No prices, untested package pins or universal performance winners are asserted.

The main unresolved commercial question is which combination makes Legion's target teams switch and keep using it. Validate that with real customer workflows before adding more frontier scope. A useful first pilot is a multi-file change containing a dirty dependency and a failing test: compare successful review, diagnosis, recovery, time and cost against the team's incumbent tools.
